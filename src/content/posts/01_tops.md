In a rare case of bipartisanship, the Democratic congress led by Charles R. McDonald teamed with Republican businessman Patrick Taylor 
to promote the "Taylor Plan", which would later become TOPS. The plan was simple: making education more affordable by completely waiving
tuition (sometimes on top of a generous stipend). Of course, the tapestry of Louisiana has changed rapidly since 1989 when the plan was first drafted. 

While states like Georgia fund in-state scholarships via the state's lottery system, TOPS is completely funded by the government, costing
around 270$ million per year. Which that price tag, many congressman has called into question the return on investment in Louisiana's 
elite students. 

As a researcher, I believe in using data tools for social good. Having grown up in Louisiana, I have seen how beneficial TOPS is. The 
scholarship can be the difference between just eking out a living during college to thriving. Additionally, the award is sometimes the 
deciding factor on whether or not some bright students attend college at all. Because TOPS is a strictly merit-based scholarship, some 
have questioned whether recipients actually need the tuition waiver, preferring the money to go to other need-based scholarship programs.
I believe that there, too, TOPS serves a purpose: if an individual achieves success due to Louisiana's K-12 investment, shouldn't the state
try to retain that talent? Indeed, the Board of Regents themselves have acknowledged that the state has a serious brain drain problem.
