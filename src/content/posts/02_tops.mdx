<h2> The Technical Magic </h2>

  &emsp; One semi-hurdle was extracting tables from a PDF file. Having worked mostly with <i>.csv</i> files or <i>.xlsx</i> files, I was used to the data processing step in data science being very minimal, a tidyr function call here, a dplyr function call there, and so on. To successfully read Louisiana's published data on TOPS disbursement[^4], I used [`tabulapdf`](https://docs.ropensci.org/tabulapdf/) to scrape the table. 

```r
processing <- function(file,year){
  out <- tabulapdf::extract_tables(file, pages = 2, col_names = FALSE) |>
    map(as_tibble)
    
  raw <- out[[1]] |>
    slice(-(1))
  
  processed <- raw |> 
    row_to_names(row_number = 1) |> 
    clean_names() |> 
    # keep only the relevant rows
    slice(c(1:6), c(12:20)) |> 
    # add derived columns
    mutate(
      college_system = ifelse(str_detect(college, "LSU"), "LSU", "UL"),
      year           = year
    ) |> 
    # drop unwanted columns
    select(-starts_with("stipend")) |> 
    select(-starts_with("natl")) |> 
    select(-starts_with("total")) |> 
    select(-matches("_4$|_5$")) |> 
    # rename by suffix -> prefix
    rename_with(~ case_when(
      str_detect(.x, "_3$") ~ paste0("honors_",      str_remove(.x, "_3$")),
      str_detect(.x, "_2$") ~ paste0("performance_", str_remove(.x, "_2$")),
      TRUE                  ~ paste0("opportunity_", .x)
    )) |>
    # pivot for a united tuition and student count column
    pivot_longer(
      cols = !c(opportunity_college_system, opportunity_year, opportunity_college),
      names_to = c("scholarship_type",".value"),
      names_pattern = "^([^_]+)_(.*)$"
    ) |>
    # rename for clean names
    rename_with(~ str_remove(.x, "^opportunity_"))
}
```

Luckily, the TOPS analysis only required looking into two school systems: the Louisiana State University system and the University of Louisiana system, as these two combined make up more than half of total TOPS scholars every year. Additionally, the PDF was published in a way that allowed for easy table reading, so the code in R was very simple. 

The <b>`processing()`</b> function on the left should produce a tidy dataframe that is ready to perform analysis on. For reasons I'll discuss later on, I ended up not creating statistical models for the data. Instead, I tried my hand at creating simple, informative graphs. 

For graphing, I typically use [`ggplot2`](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/). I know, I know. Asking a data scientist which graphing tool to use is like asking whether Coke is better than Pepsi. I've used seaborn and matplotlib before, and while ggplot's geometry layering can be hard to learn, I think it's easier to manipulate once you get the hang of it[^5].

<h2> Lessons Learned </h2>


  &emsp; Starting something new can be challenging, but what makes it easier is starting small. I thought long and hard what I wanted to first post to be, and I chose a simple R project because I'm familiar with the <b>`tidyverse`</b> workflow and new I could quickly create something simple, cute, and interesting. I tinkered around with substack and tumblr, but I knew I wanted to create my own frontend for ultimate customizability, so the project's simplicity allowed me to focus my efforts on web design.
  
  With that being said, this TOPS project has deepened my respect for researchers. The post is roughly a 10 minute read, but it took much longer to research. I wanted to represent the pros and cons of the Taylor scholarship as accurately as possible and remain bipartisan myself since TOPS was a bipartisan effort. As a result, I dug through many legal documents (none of which was neatly documented) and read countless news articles interviewing state representatives about their opinion of the Taylor Program. I went back to the original vision and found myself resonating with an oil magnate whose company is responsible for one of the biggest oil spills in the country [^6]. That was certainly a surprise. 
  
  I also learned some not-so-nice things. I learned that the International High School of New Orleans shut down due to not enough students meeting the state's accountability standards. A few of my friends went to that school. I also learned that Louisiana has <b>the highest poverty rate</b> in the country and that not enough of those students receive the need-based GO Grant.
  
  One important thing I have learned is that not everything will model nicely. Although it is my goal to provide an elegant data-based solutions to real problems, some issues requires more care and attention. The effectiveness of TOPS is one of them.
